Part I

Summary

The proposition, the numbers that matter, and the three findings that change how this business should be pitched.

1 · Executive Summary

The proposition

FROLIC is a premium prebiotic soda brand for urban India, built on Indian flavour idiom rather than a Western one. Ten SKUs drawn from regional drinking culture — nimbu masala, aam panna, kokum, jamun, kala khatta, jeera — each carrying 7 g of prebiotic fibre and roughly a quarter of the added sugar of a conventional soft drink, at an MRP of ₹130 for a 250 ml can.

The category has been proven decisively outside India. Poppi was acquired by PepsiCo in May 2025 for $1.95 bn in cash plus $0.2 bn contingent — approximately 3.9× its 2024 revenue of ~$500 m, as disclosed in PepsiCo's Form 10-Q. Olipop was valued at $1.85 bn and has been profitable since early 2024. Both were founded in 2018. The Indian equivalent category was worth approximately $12.6 m in 2025 — roughly 0.2% of the functional beverage market it sits inside.

1.1 Three findings that change the pitch

This report was built by verifying the load-bearing inputs rather than accepting them. Three came back differently from how they are usually presented, and each materially changes the investment case.

Finding 1 — the first-mover claim is false

India already has at least two prebiotic soda brands in market. Misfits (Mumbai) is seed-funded by Nu Ventures alongside angels who backed Acko and Third Wave Coffee, and sells through its own site, Amazon India and Swiggy Instamart. Its specification is 250 ml, 7 g prebiotic fibre, 4 g sugar — within rounding error of FROLIC's — at a lower price point, and with zero added sugar against FROLIC's 4 g. Bubz is a second entrant.

Any pitch claiming "India's first" fails the moment a partner runs one search. The reframe is stronger anyway: category creation with two sub-scale incumbents beats true first-mover, because someone else is funding consumer education and nobody has won distribution. FROLIC's wedge is not primacy — it is being the only brand in the category that tastes Indian.

Finding 2 — a 40% GST slab governs the entire economic model

From 22 September 2025 India taxes aerated sweetened beverages at a flat 40% GST, replacing the earlier 28% plus 12% cess. At an MRP of ₹130, ₹37.14 is tax — 29% of shelf price — before the brand, distributor or retailer is paid anything.

This sets the price floor (minimum viable MRP is ₹115.89, leaving only 11% of discount headroom), makes D2C permanently the most profitable channel, and rules out price-led competition for every participant. Non-carbonated beverages sit at 5% — a 35-point lever held on the Series A roadmap.

Finding 3 — the exit multiple is observed, not chosen

The return model applies 3.9× revenue, which is the multiple PepsiCo actually paid for Poppi per its SEC filing — not a comparable-company average. Using a strategic acquirer's disclosed multiple for the same category is considerably more defensible than a constructed comp set.

This contradicts the stated ask

Do not raise ₹10 Cr now. Investor readiness today assesses at 5.7 / 10, dragged down by zero traction and — more seriously — an undocumented founding team. 4 · Product & Formulation

4.1 What the product is

A carbonated soft drink in a 250 ml sleek aluminium can, containing 7 g of prebiotic fibre and between 2 g and 5 g of added sugar depending on flavour. Caffeine-free across the entire range. Shelf-stable at ambient temperature for a target 12 months.

The functional premise rests on prebiotic fibre, not live cultures. A prebiotic is defined by what happens to it rather than what it is made of: it must resist digestion in the upper gut, be fermentable by colonic micro-organisms, and selectively favour some of them. Inulin — the fibre in chicory root — satisfies all three, because its fructose units are joined by bonds human enzymes cannot cleave.

A structural advantage over probiotic competitors

Because FROLIC contains no live cultures, it is shelf-stable and requires no cold chain. It survives carbonation, canning, and a warm delivery van. Probiotic products carry refrigeration requirements and short shelf lives that make national distribution in India materially harder and more expensive.

4.2 The fibre blend

A 60:40 ratio of chicory inulin to acacia gum, delivering 7 g per can. Single-source fibre is simpler; a blend performs better. Inulin ferments relatively quickly, acacia gum slowly and further along the colon. Running them together spreads fermentation out, which in practice means materially better tolerance for consumers who are not used to a high-fibre drink.

This matters commercially as well as physiologically. Any meaningful increase in fibre intake can cause temporary bloating, and a poor first week is how a consumer concludes that fibre is not for them. The blend, and the explicit "introduce gradually" advisory on pack, are both retention measures.

4.3 The formulation problem

Putting 7 g of fibre into a clear carbonated liquid is the technical barrier that has kept this category small. Inulin clouds the liquid and drops out of suspension under carbonation; acacia adds body that is unwanted in citrus formulations; carbonation strips the volatile aromatics that make fresh ginger and lime rind taste fresh rather than flat.

The mitigations are cold-pressing and cold-holding aromatic fractions, adding them late in the process, and the 60:40 blend ratio. None of this has been validated by a production run. Every cost and stability figure in this report is an estimate until one is completed.

4.4 Where FROLIC is and is not differentiated

Genuinely differentiated

  • Flavour portfolio — ten SKUs from regional Indian drinking culture. A foreign entrant cannot manufacture this; an Indian incumbent could, but has not.
  • Brand craft — materially ahead of the category.
  • Seasonal sourcing — jamun and kokum are hard to secure at scale, which is a barrier to fast followers.
  • Direct relationship — subscription and rewards built from day one.

Not differentiated

  • The fibre specification — 7 g / 4 g is table stakes and already matched by Misfits.
  • The format — 250 ml sleek can is category standard.
  • The health claim — weaker than the incumbent's zero-added-sugar position.
  • Anything operational — no plant, no distribution, no supply advantage.

The comparison set that matters

FROLIC's competition is not Coca-Cola. At ₹130 with a 40% tax, FROLIC and a ₹20 cola are not in the same purchase decision. The "India is price-sensitive" objection is true of the mass market and not of this consumer.

7.3 Why India is underpenetrated

  1. The category was economically impossible until recently. Food-grade prebiotic fibre at scale was import-dependent and expensive, and the formulation problem is genuinely hard.
  2. Health-led beverage in India has been dairy-led. Yakult trained a generation on "gut health = a small dairy shot". Nobody translated that into the soda occasion, where the volume is.
  3. Kombucha taught the wrong lesson. Incumbents read its stall as "Indians do not want functional fizz". The correct reading is narrower: Indians did not want vinegary functional fizz at ₹200.
  4. The 40% GST slab deters volume players. A brand competing on price is structurally dead, which has kept the category clear of exactly the players who would otherwise crush a startup.
  5. Distribution is not yet contested. Quick commerce has compressed time-to-consumer from years to months, and neither incumbent holds national retail.

7.4 Seasonality

Indian beverage demand is summer-weighted, concentrated in March to June. Working capital planning assumes a Q1 inventory build ahead of that peak. Jamun carries an additional constraint: a roughly six-week harvest window and poor fresh shelf life, requiring freezing at harvest and forward contracting.

Part IV

Operations

How the product gets made, cleared, sold and delivered — and the regulatory constraints that shape all three.

11 · Manufacturing & Supply Chain

11.1 Manufacturing model

Co-packing (tolling) through Year 3. This is the standard and correct model for a beverage startup: it converts a large fixed capital requirement into a variable per-unit cost, and removes the risk of owning idle capacity while demand is unproven. Own capacity becomes a Series B consideration, and only if volume justifies it.

The principal risk of tolling is capacity priority during the March–June peak, when every beverage brand in India wants the same line. The mitigation is committed annual volume contracts, which are also the lever that drives the conversion charge down from ₹5.50 to ₹2.90 per can across the projection period.

Not yet in place

No co-packer has been signed and no production run has been completed. Both are month 0–2 milestones and prerequisites for the seed round. Every cost figure in Part V is an estimate until one production run converts it into an invoice.

There is nothing meaningful to save by reducing fibre, and doing so would destroy the proposition. The real cost lever is the can body, and it is a volume negotiation.

12.1 Tax treatment — the governing constraint

From 22 September 2025 India taxes aerated and sweetened beverages at a flat 40% GST, replacing the previous 28% plus 12% compensation cess. Non-carbonated fruit- and juice-based drinks and packaged water sit at 5%.

40%GST on aerated sweetened beverages V

5%GST on non-carbonated fruit-based drinks V
35 ptThe arbitrage between the two slabs
A 35-point strategic lever

A still (non-carbonated) FROLIC — same fibre, same Indian flavours, no carbonation — would face 5% GST. At the same ₹130 MRP the ex-tax value rises from ₹92.86 to ₹123.81: +₹30.95 per can, straight into the value chain. That is larger than the entire Year-5 COGS reduction programme, achievable immediately, and requires no scale.

This is not a recommendation to abandon carbonation — soda is the category and the product premise. It is a higher-margin second act, a hedge against further tightening of sin-goods policy, and a route into HoReCa and institutional channels where carbonation is a drawback. It belongs on the Series A roadmap.

12.3 How compliance is enforced in the build

  • Ingredient copy fields carry the discipline explicitly: every description states what the ingredient is or does in the can, never an outcome for the drinker.
  • The CMS schema rejects a banned-phrase list at authoring time with an actionable error message.
  • Nutrition panels validate against arithmetic impossibilities — added sugar cannot exceed total sugar, prebiotic fibre cannot exceed dietary fibre.
  • Compliance sign-off is a blocking gate on any nutrition or ingredient copy change, with full document history as the audit trail.
  • The legal disclaimer appears in the global footer of all 37 pages, not on a policy page.

12.4 Label requirements per can

FSSAI licence number and logo; veg mark; nutrition panel per 100 ml and per serving; full ingredient list in descending order by weight; allergen declaration; net quantity and MRP inclusive of all taxes; batch or lot number, date of manufacture and best-before; manufacturer name and address; customer care contact; storage instruction. Plus one item not required by regulation:

"Contains added dietary fibre. Introduce gradually." This is a voluntary advisory. A poor first week is how a consumer decides fibre is not for them, and telling people up front costs less than a refund and a one-star review.

13.2 Field ownership

A product record is the union of two sources, with exclusive field ownership — no field is writable from both systems. Shopify owns price, SKU, variant ID and inventory; Sanity owns story, flavour world, nutrition and quiz taxonomy. Duplicating a price would create two numbers that can disagree, and the one the customer sees would not be the one they are charged.

13.3 Engineering decisions with commercial consequences

  • The 3D can is generated, not modelled. Lathe geometry plus a canvas-drawn label and a procedural lighting rig. A new flavour ships without any 3D work, and nothing in the render path can fail over the network.
  • three.js is genuinely conditional. Capability and viewport gates sit outside the dynamic-import boundary, so the renderer is never fetched on reduced-motion, Save-Data, slow-network, low-core or low-memory devices. First Load JS is 170 kB against 405 kB before that change.
  • Bundle lines carry their own unit price. A four-can share of a 24-can box is not re-priced as a four-can pack, so the cart total always matches the price shown.
  • Chart colours are a separate validated set from brand primaries, checked for colour-vision separation and 3:1 contrast.

13.4 Known gaps

Stated plainly, because they will surface in technical diligence: checkout is not wired to Shopify; forms do not submit; reviews are illustrative placeholders and the aggregate-rating structured data they feed must be disabled before launch; there is no test suite; and no analytics are instrumented.

21.1 The three that matter most

Risk 1 — taste does not win

FROLIC's only durable differentiation is flavour, and it is entirely unproven. Misfits ships a cleaner health claim (zero added sugar). Mitigation: run the blind test before spending anything else. ~₹4 L, six weeks. If FROLIC loses, reformulate rather than fundraise.

Risk 2 — the incumbent raises first

Misfits is already funded. A Series A that locks national distribution closes the window before FROLIC has a product in market.

Mitigation: compress time to first production run. The window is 18–30 months and every month spent fundraising instead of manufacturing consumes it.

Risk 3 — the team gap

No FMCG distribution capability is documented. Indian beverage distribution cannot be built from a cold start, and no amount of capital substitutes for the relationships.

Mitigation: hire or co-found before the seed round. This is the single highest-leverage action available to the project.

Annexure C · Investor FAQ — Selected

The full set of 100 questions is maintained in docs/investor/09-investor-faq.md. Reproduced here are those most likely to be asked in a first meeting.

Market

How big is the Indian prebiotic soda category?

~USD 12.6 m in 2025 — roughly 0.2% of the functional beverage market it sits inside. That is both the opportunity and the risk in one figure.

Isn’t that too small to build a venture business in?

Today, yes. The bet is on trajectory: the US equivalent went from zero to two brands worth ~$3.8 bn combined in seven years.

Isn’t India too price-sensitive for a ₹130 can?

The mass market is; this consumer is not. They already pay ₹125 for Red Bull and ₹250 for specialty coffee. We are mid-range in that set, and should never be compared to a ₹20 cola.

Who exactly is the customer?

Adults 18–45, SEC A/A+, in five metros, already spending ₹100+ on a single beverage. 2.99 million people — not "health-conscious Indians".

Competition

Are you claiming to be India’s first prebiotic soda?

No, and any deck that does is wrong. Misfits and Bubz are both in market; Misfits is seed-funded with a near-identical specification.

Then what is your differentiation?

Indian flavour idiom. Misfits is a Western-format minimal-ingredient soda made in India. FROLIC is nimbu masala, aam panna, kokum, jamun and jeera.

Misfits has zero added sugar. You have 4 g. Isn’t that a disadvantage?

Yes, and we disclose it. Our position is that 4 g buys meaningfully better taste and that taste drives repeat more than a label figure. This must be proven blind before the seed round, not asserted.

What stops Coca-Cola or PepsiCo from crushing you?

Nothing, eventually. But their advantages are cost, scale and price — none of which help in a 40%-taxed premium category. Their realistic move is to buy the category leader, as PepsiCo did with Poppi.

Margins & economics

What is your gross margin?

55.7% in Year 1 rising to 66.7% by Year 5, on net revenue after 40% GST. Modelled, not yet validated by a production run.

What do you actually net per can?

₹84.01 D2C, ₹67.14 quick commerce, ₹61.57 modern trade — from a ₹130 MRP. ₹37.14 of every can is tax.

What is LTV:CAC?

1.8× in Year 1, crossing 3× only in Year 3. Below the threshold most consumer investors require, and we disclose it rather than presenting a blended figure that hides it.

What is the most important number in the model?

90-day repeat rate. Modelled at 34% in Year 1. At 25% the business needs a fourth round and LTV:CAC never clears 3×.

How much does the prebiotic fibre cost?

₹4.00 per can — 12% of COGS. The aluminium can costs three times more than the functional ingredient.

Manufacturing & regulation

Do you own a plant?

No. Co-packed through Year 3. Own capacity is a Series B consideration, and only if volume justifies it.

Can you actually make this product?

The formulation challenge is real — 7 g of fibre in a clear carbonated liquid clouds and drops out of suspension. No production run has happened yet.

Do you need cold chain?

No. Shelf-stable ambient, because we use prebiotic fibre rather than live cultures. A genuine structural advantage over probiotic competitors.

What health claims can you legally make?

Composition claims only. "Contains 7 g of prebiotic fibre" is fine; "supports gut health" is a prohibited health claim under FSS (Advertising & Claims) Regulations 2018.

Fundraising & exit

How much are you raising?

The recommendation in this report is not to raise ₹10 Cr now. Raise ₹1.5–2.5 Cr from angels against a production run and six months of D2C, then the seed on cohort data.

Why not raise the full seed now?

Readiness is 5.7/10 — zero traction and an undocumented team. Raising on a model, against a funded incumbent, invites a pass or a valuation that damages the cap table permanently.

What multiple are you using for exit?

3.9× revenue — the multiple PepsiCo actually paid for Poppi per its Form 10-Q, not a comparable-company average.

What does seed return?

15.9× conservative, 39.0× base, 74.5× aggressive. Probability-weighted with a 55% failure rate: 13.6× MOIC, 45.1% IRR.

Is an IPO realistic?

Not at these numbers. ₹760 Cr of Year 7 revenue is below the scale at which an Indian consumer IPO makes sense. Trade sale is the base case.
