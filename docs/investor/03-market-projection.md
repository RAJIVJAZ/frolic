## Bottom-up, not top-down

A "0.5% of an $8bn market" slide is worthless — it assumes the conclusion.
This model builds from people.

```
Adults 18–45 across Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Pune, Hyderabad
                                                        46.0 M
        × SEC A/A+ share                      26%   →   11.96 M
        × already buy a ₹100+ beverage        25%   →    2.99 M
                                                    ─────────────
        BEACHHEAD (5 metros)                            2.99 M
```

**2.99 million people.** Not 1.4 billion, not "urban India". This is the
population FROLIC must win before national expansion is even a question, and
every customer number below is a percentage of it.

The relevant comparison set for this consumer is **not** Coca-Cola. It is
Red Bull (₹125), a specialty coffee (₹250), kombucha (₹150–250) and
cold-pressed juice (₹120). FROLIC at ₹130 is mid-range in that set.

## Repeat purchase — the assumption that decides everything

| Year | Annual retention | Orders/customer/yr | 3-yr LTV | CAC | LTV:CAC | Payback |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Y1 | 34% | 2.4 | ₹1,506 | ₹850 | **1.8×** | 10.0 mo |
| Y2 | 38% | 2.5 | ₹1,738 | ₹780 | **2.2×** | 8.3 mo |
| Y3 | 42% | 2.6 | ₹2,000 | ₹640 | **3.1×** | 6.2 mo |
| Y4 | 45% | 2.7 | ₹2,226 | ₹580 | **3.8×** | 5.2 mo |
| Y5 | 47% | 2.8 | ₹2,513 | ₹540 | **4.7×** | 4.4 mo |

> **Read this honestly.** Year 1 LTV:CAC of **1.8×** is below the 3× floor
> most consumer investors require. On a three-year view FROLIC acquires its
> first two cohorts at close to breakeven. The business only becomes
> attractive on a unit basis in Year 3, when CAC falls and repeat rises
> together.
>
> This is normal for a brand buying its first customers — but it means the
> seed round is explicitly funding *the journey to good unit economics*, not
> scaling economics that already work. Any investor told otherwise will
> discover it in diligence.

The two levers that matter more than anything else in this model:

1. **Annual retention.** At 34% (Y1) the model works. At 25% it does not —
   LTV:CAC never clears 3× and the business needs a fourth round.
2. **Cans per order.** At 10 cans the ₹4.20/can D2C last-mile is absorbable.
   At 6 cans it is ₹8.40/can and Year 1 gross margin drops below 50%. This is
   the entire reason the bundle builder enforces 12- and 24-can boxes.

## Geographic sequencing

| Phase | Period | Markets | Channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Y1 | Lucknow, Delhi NCR | D2C + quick commerce only |
| 2 | Y2 | + Mumbai, Bengaluru, Pune, Hyderabad | + modern trade, HoReCa |
| 3 | Y3 | Top 5 consolidated | + early general trade |
| 4 | Y4 | + Chennai, Kolkata, Ahmedabad, Jaipur, Chandigarh | Full omnichannel |
| 5 | Y5 | Pan-India metro + tier-1 | National distribution |

Two cities in Year 1, not five. Concentration beats coverage when the goal is
shelf velocity — a brand that sells 8 units/store/week in 200 stores in one city
is fundable; the same volume spread across 1,000 stores in five cities is not.

**Phase 1 follows the plant, not the biggest market.** From the Prayagraj base,
Lucknow (~200 km) is a low-cost proving ground and Delhi NCR (~600 km) is the
first large premium market on a single truck route. Mumbai and Bengaluru move to
Year 2. The beachhead sizing is unchanged — manufacturing location does not move
where the customers are. See [10-siting-and-sourcing.md](10-siting-and-sourcing.md).

## Where this projection is most likely wrong

| Assumption | Risk |
|---|---|
| Y5 reaches 40% of the beachhead | **High.** 40% penetration of a defined cohort in 5 years is aggressive for any consumer brand |
| 34% Y1 retention | **High.** Entirely unvalidated. The single most important number in the pack |
| 10+ cans per order | **Medium.** Depends on bundle mechanics working as designed |
| ₹850 Y1 CAC | **Medium.** Indian D2C CAC for premium F&B is typically ₹700–1,400 |
| Channel mix shift to retail by Y4 | **Medium.** Requires distributor relationships that do not yet exist |
