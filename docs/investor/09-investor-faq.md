# 09 · Investor FAQ — 100 Questions

Answers are deliberately short. Where the honest answer is "we don't know
yet", it says so — an investor discovers that in diligence anyway, and
discovering it *after* being told otherwise is what kills deals.

---

## A. Market (1–12)

**1. How big is the Indian functional beverage market?**
USD 4.2–8.3 bn (2025–26) depending on category definition, growing ~10–11%
CAGR. The range is wide because "functional" is not a standardised category.
We underwrite against a bottom-up beachhead, not this number.

**2. How big is prebiotic soda specifically in India?**
~USD 12.6 mn in 2025 — roughly 0.2% of the functional market it sits inside.
That is the opportunity and the risk in one figure.

**3. Isn't a $12.6 mn category too small to build a venture business in?**
Today, yes. The bet is on the trajectory: the US equivalent went from zero to
two brands worth ~$3.8 bn combined in seven years. If India follows at even a
fraction of that rate, the category supports a ₹1,000 Cr+ business.

**4. What if it doesn't follow?**
Then FROLIC is a ₹73 Cr revenue premium beverage brand at Year 5 with ₹4 Cr
of EBITDA — our conservative case. Seed still returns ~15.9×; Series B
returns 1.1×. The downside is survivable for early money and poor for late.

**5. Who exactly is the customer?**
Adults 18–45, SEC A/A+, in five metros, who already spend ₹100+ on a single
beverage — coffee, energy drink, kombucha, cold-pressed juice. 2.99 million
people. Not "health-conscious Indians".

**6. Why five metros and not national?**
Shelf velocity beats shelf coverage. 8 units/store/week across 200 Mumbai
stores is fundable; the same volume across 1,000 stores in five cities is
not. We start in two cities, not five.

**7. Is the market big enough at that narrow a definition?**
2.99 M people × ₹2,226 ARPU = ~₹665 Cr of addressable spend in the beachhead
alone, before any national expansion. We model reaching 40% of it by Year 5,
which is aggressive.

**8. Isn't India too price-sensitive for a ₹130 can?**
The mass market is. Our consumer is not — they already pay ₹125 for Red Bull
and ₹250 for a specialty coffee. We are mid-range in that set. We are not
competing with a ₹20 Coke and should never be compared to one.

**9. What's the real TAM if this works?**
Urban premium beverage across the top 20 Indian cities. We deliberately do
not publish a top-down TAM — it would be a made-up number.

**10. How does seasonality affect the business?**
Significantly. Beverage in India is summer-weighted (Mar–Jun). Working
capital planning assumes a Q1 inventory build. Jamun has a six-week harvest
window, which constrains that SKU specifically.

**11. Is gut health a fad in India?**
Awareness is real and mainstream in metros — Yakult trained a generation.
Whether it converts to a soda occasion at ₹130 is the actual open question,
and only repeat data answers it.

**12. What happens to the category if GST policy changes?**
Downside: further tightening on sin goods compresses everyone equally.
Upside: a still (non-carbonated) line faces 5% GST vs 40% — a 35-point
lever we hold in reserve.

## B. Competition (13–26)

**13. Aren't you claiming to be India's first prebiotic soda?**
No, and any deck that does is wrong. Misfits and Bubz are both in market.
Misfits is seed-funded, sells on its own site, Amazon and Swiggy Instamart.

**14. Then what's your differentiation?**
Indian flavour idiom. Misfits is a Western-format minimal-ingredient soda
made in India. FROLIC is nimbu masala, aam panna, kokum, jamun, jeera — ten
SKUs drawn from regional drinking culture.

**15. Is that actually defensible?**
Flavour formulation is genuinely hard and takes years. Seasonal sourcing of
jamun and kokum is harder still. Brand craft compounds. Those three together
are the moat. The fibre spec is not.

**16. Misfits has 7g fibre and 4g sugar too. How is your product different?**
On spec, it isn't. That's precisely why we don't lead with spec. The
difference is what it tastes like and what it reminds you of.

**17. Misfits has zero added sugar. You have 4g. Isn't that a disadvantage?**
Yes. On the health axis we are behind, and we disclose it. Our position is
that 4 g buys meaningfully better taste, and that taste drives repeat more
than a sugar line on a label. **This must be proven in a blind test before
the seed round**, not asserted.

**18. What if Misfits raises a big Series A first?**
That is the primary competitive risk. It is why the window is 18–30 months
and why the current recommendation is to move on product proof now rather
than spend six months fundraising.

**19. What stops Coca-Cola or PepsiCo from crushing you?**
Nothing, eventually. Their advantages are cost, scale and price — none of
which help in a 40%-taxed premium category. Their realistic move is to buy
the category leader, as PepsiCo did with Poppi.

**20. PepsiCo already owns Poppi. Why wouldn't they just launch it here?**
They might. Our defence is that Poppi's flavour portfolio is Western and
localising it means building the Indian flavour library we would already
own. That's a real barrier, not an insurmountable one.

**21. Why hasn't Paper Boat done this?**
Paper Boat proved Indian traditional flavours scale as a premium brand and
was acquired by Wipro. They have the flavour credibility and no functional
claim. They are the most likely domestic fast-follower.

**22. Why did kombucha stall in India?**
Taste and price. Vinegary at ₹200. Prebiotic soda solves both — it tastes
like soda and costs ₹130. Incumbents read kombucha's stall as "Indians don't
want functional fizz", which we think is the wrong lesson.

**23. What's your response if a competitor undercuts you on price?**
We can't follow far. Minimum viable MRP at a 50% GM floor is ₹115.89 against
our ₹130 — 11% headroom. But the same arithmetic binds them. Nobody can buy
this market with price.

**24. Isn't 11% headroom dangerously thin?**
It is a genuine constraint. It rules out BOGO and deep discounting as growth
levers, which forces us to compete on brand and taste. We regard that as the
competition we'd choose.

**25. How do you compete with Red Bull for the same ₹130?**
Different occasion. Red Bull is caffeine and alertness; FROLIC is zero
caffeine and digestive positioning. We overlap on price, not on need state.

**26. Who else could enter?**
Any funded D2C brand with a co-packer relationship. Barriers to entry are
low; barriers to *scale* — distribution, brand, flavour library — are not.

## C. Product & Margins (27–42)

**27. What's your gross margin?**
55.7% in Year 1 rising to 66.7% by Year 5, on net revenue after 40% GST.
Modelled, not yet validated by a production run.

**28. Why does gross margin improve so much?**
COGS falls ₹34 → ₹23 per can as we move from co-packer tolling to committed
annual can procurement. It is a procurement outcome, not a pricing one.

**29. What does a can actually cost to make?**
Year 1: ₹34. Ingredients ₹12.50, can body ₹12, can end ₹1.50, secondary
packaging ₹2.50, conversion ₹5.50.

**30. How much does the prebiotic fibre cost?**
₹4.00 per can — 12% of COGS. The aluminium can costs three times more than
the functional ingredient.

**31. So could you cut fibre to save money?**
There's nothing meaningful to save, and it would destroy the product premise.
The cost lever is the can, and it's a volume negotiation.

**32. What do you actually net per can?**
Depends entirely on channel: ₹84.01 D2C, ₹67.14 quick commerce, ₹61.57
modern trade — from a ₹130 MRP.

**33. Why is the spread so wide?**
₹37.14 of every can is GST. After that, quick commerce takes 25% commission
and modern trade takes ~31% in combined retailer and distributor margin.

**34. Which channel do you actually want?**
D2C — 36% more per can than modern trade, and it is the only channel that
gives us the customer relationship. Retail is brand-building that happens to
roughly break even.

**35. Then why do retail at all?**
Visibility, credibility and the volume needed to get COGS down. A premium
beverage that exists only online has a ceiling.

**36. What's your CAC?**
Modelled at ₹850 Year 1, falling to ₹540 by Year 5. Unvalidated. Indian D2C
CAC for premium F&B typically runs ₹700–1,400.

**37. What's LTV:CAC?**
1.8× in Year 1, crossing 3× only in Year 3. Below the threshold most
consumer investors require, and we disclose it rather than presenting a
blended figure that hides it.

**38. That's a weak Year 1. Why should we fund it?**
Because the seed is explicitly funding *the journey to good unit economics*,
not scaling economics that already work. Any investor told otherwise finds
out in diligence.

**39. What's CAC payback?**
10.0 months in Year 1, 4.4 months by Year 5.

**40. What's the most important number in the whole model?**
90-day repeat rate. Modelled at 34% Year 1. At 25% the business needs a
fourth round and LTV:CAC never clears 3×.

**41. Why does basket size matter so much?**
D2C last-mile is ~₹50/order. At 10 cans that's ₹4.20/can and absorbable; at
6 cans it's ₹8.40/can and Year 1 gross margin drops below 50%. It's why the
bundle builder enforces 12- and 24-can boxes.

**42. Why 250 ml and not a larger format?**
330 ml carries 1.1 points better margin, which doesn't justify a second SKU's
tooling, inventory and shelf complexity pre-revenue. 250 ml also matches the
price point consumers accept and the incumbent's format. 330 ml comes in
Year 3.

## D. Manufacturing & Supply (43–55)

**43. Do you own a plant?**
No. Co-packed through Year 3. Own capacity is a Series B consideration, and
only if volume justifies it.

**44. Isn't co-packing risky?**
It's capital-efficient and the standard model for beverage startups. The risk
is capacity priority during peak season, mitigated by committed volume
contracts.

**45. Have you signed a co-packer?**
Not yet. This is a month 0–2 milestone and a prerequisite for the seed.

**46. Can you actually make this product?**
The formulation challenge is real — 7 g of fibre in a clear, stable,
carbonated liquid clouds, drops out of suspension and changes mouthfeel. The
brand story describes eleven formulation rounds. **No production run has
happened yet.**

**47. What's the shelf life?**
Target 12 months ambient. Requires accelerated and real-time validation
before retail. Not yet done.

**48. Where do you source prebiotic fibre?**
Chicory inulin and acacia gum, both available domestically or via established
import routes. 60:40 inulin-to-acacia blend.

**49. Why a blend rather than pure inulin?**
Inulin ferments fast, which some people feel. Acacia ferments slowly and
further along the colon. The blend spreads fermentation out — materially
better tolerance for people new to high-fibre drinks.

**50. Are jamun and kokum actually sourceable at scale?**
This is a genuine operational risk. Jamun has a ~six-week season and poor
shelf life; kokum grows on one coastline. Both require freezing at harvest
and forward contracting. It's also a barrier to fast followers.

**51. What's your minimum order quantity?**
Typical Indian co-packer MOQs run 30,000–50,000 cans per SKU per run. This is
the main argument for launching 3 SKUs, not 10.

**52. Ten SKUs at launch seems like a lot.**
It is, and we'd expect any consumer investor to push back. The recommendation
in this pack is to launch with three and expand on demonstrated velocity.

**53. What about quality control and recall?**
Batch coding, retained samples, documented recall procedure, and third-party
lab testing per batch. Required before retail, not optional.

**54. What's the working capital cycle?**
~55 days of COGS tied up in inventory and channel receivables at peak. Peak
working capital is ₹13.4 Cr in the base case, ₹29.8 Cr in the aggressive
case.

**55. Why does the aggressive case need more capital than the base case?**
Growth consumes working capital. Cans are manufactured, shipped and sit on
shelves months before cash returns. Growing faster is a cash-hungry outcome,
not a cash-generating one.

## E. Regulation & Compliance (56–65)

**56. What's the GST treatment?**
40% flat slab on aerated sweetened beverages, effective 22 September 2025,
replacing 28% + 12% cess.

**57. How much does that cost you?**
₹37.14 on a ₹130 can — 29% of shelf price before anyone in the value chain
is paid.

**58. Is there any way around it?**
Not for a carbonated product. A non-carbonated line faces 5% GST — a
35-point difference worth ₹30.95 per can at the same MRP. It's on the Series
A roadmap.

**59. Then why not launch still instead of carbonated?**
Carbonation is the category and the product premise — "soda" is what makes
this a mainstream occasion rather than a wellness shot. The still line is a
second act, not a replacement.

**60. What health claims can you legally make in India?**
Composition claims only — "contains 7 g of prebiotic fibre" is fine.
"Supports gut health", "improves digestion", "boosts immunity" are
prohibited health claims under FSS (Advertising & Claims) Regulations 2018.

**61. How do you enforce that?**
The CMS schemas reject a banned-phrase list at authoring time, nutrition
panels validate against arithmetic impossibilities, and compliance sign-off
is a blocking gate on any nutrition or ingredient copy change.

**62. Isn't under-claiming a marketing disadvantage?**
It's a compliance requirement and, we'd argue, a brand asset. The category
has an overselling problem. Being the brand that told you less than it could
is a position nobody else is taking.

**63. What FSSAI approvals do you need?**
Manufacturing licence (or the co-packer's), product approval, label approval,
and nutrition panel verification. Plus batch-level compliance.

**64. Any risk from the "sin goods" classification?**
Reputational and regulatory. The industry is lobbying for reclassification.
We model no relief.

**65. What about advertising regulation?**
ASCI code applies. No health outcomes in paid creative including influencer
briefs, `#ad` disclosure mandatory, and under the Consumer Protection Act
2019 the endorser carries liability too.

## F. Distribution (66–76)

**66. How do you get distribution?**
Phase 1 D2C and quick commerce only. Modern trade from Year 2. General trade
from Year 3. National from Year 5.

**67. Why quick commerce so early?**
It reaches the exact target consumer in minutes and has compressed time-to-
market from years to months. 25% commission is expensive but cheaper to serve
than modern trade.

**68. Do you have distributor relationships?**
No. This is the largest operational gap and the reason a co-founder or first
hire with FMCG distribution experience is the highest-leverage addition to
the team.

**69. What's a realistic shelf velocity target?**
4+ units/store/week sustained over 8 weeks in a 50-store pilot. Below that,
distributors delist.

**70. What margins does trade expect?**
Retailer 22–25% of MRP, distributor 8–10%. Non-negotiable in Indian FMCG.

**71. Do you need cold chain?**
No. The product is shelf-stable ambient — which is a direct consequence of
using prebiotic fibre rather than live probiotic cultures. It's a real
structural advantage over probiotic competitors.

**72. What about HoReCa?**
Cafés, gyms and offices at ~₹79/can net — second only to D2C. High-intent,
high-visibility, and a genuine trial channel. Included from Year 2.

**73. What's the COD situation?**
COD is ~35% of Indian D2C beverage orders and drives RTO losses. We model a
5% payment-and-RTO provision on D2C and cap COD at ₹2,000.

**74. How do you reduce RTO?**
Prepaid incentives, pincode-level COD restriction based on RTO history, and
WhatsApp order confirmation before dispatch.

**75. Is Amazon/Flipkart part of the plan?**
Secondary. Marketplace economics are poor for heavy, low-AOV goods and the
customer relationship is not ours.

**76. What's the biggest distribution risk?**
That quick commerce platforms raise commissions or deprioritise the category.
We'd be exposed — it's ~40% of volume by Year 3.

## G. Consumer Adoption (77–86)

**77. Where are you on the adoption curve?**
Year 1 at 0.6% of the beachhead is innovator territory. Year 3 at 8.4% is
early adopters. Year 5 at 40% is early majority.

**78. Is 40% of the beachhead by Year 5 realistic?**
It's aggressive. We flag it as one of the model's most attackable
assumptions. A more conservative 20–25% still supports a ₹150 Cr+ business.

**79. How do you get trial?**
Sampling at gyms, offices and cafés; quick commerce first-order discounts;
the flavour quiz reducing choice paralysis; and a 6-can starter pack that
makes "I don't know which one" a cheap decision.

**80. Ten flavours seems like choice paralysis.**
It is, which is why the site leads with four bestsellers and a quiz, and why
this pack recommends launching with three SKUs.

**81. What drives repeat?**
Taste, primarily. Then subscription mechanics and habit formation. We model
34% Year 1 annual retention rising to 47%.

**82. What's the biggest adoption risk?**
That Indian consumers want their nimbu soda to taste like nimbu soda and
don't want a fibre claim attached. The quadrant we're targeting may be empty
because it's small, not because nobody thought of it.

**83. How do you know people will pay ₹130?**
We don't yet. They pay it for Red Bull and more for kombucha, which is
evidence but not proof. The six-month D2C pilot answers it.

**84. What about the fibre side-effects issue?**
7 g of fibre causes bloating in people with low baseline intake. We address
it directly in product copy and on the label — "introduce gradually" — because
a bad first week is how somebody decides fibre isn't for them.

**85. Isn't warning customers bad for conversion?**
Short-term, marginally. Long-term it prevents one-star reviews and refunds.
The 4-star review on the site that says "start slow on the fibre" is
deliberately published.

**86. What's your repeat purchase assumption based on?**
Category benchmarks and the US comparables. It is not based on FROLIC data,
because none exists. This is the number to validate before raising.

## H. Fundraising (87–94)

**87. How much are you raising?**
The recommendation in this pack is **not** to raise ₹10 Cr now. Raise
₹1.5–2.5 Cr from angels against a production run and six months of D2C, then
the ₹10 Cr seed on cohort data.

**88. Why not raise the full seed now?**
Investor Readiness Score today is 5.7/10 — dragged down by zero traction and
an undocumented team. Raising on a model, against a funded incumbent, invites
a valuation that damages the cap table permanently, or a pass.

**89. What's the seed valuation?**
₹50 Cr post-money for ₹10 Cr. Entry price is the single biggest driver of
seed returns — at ₹80 Cr post, seed MOIC drops from 39× to ~24×.

**90. What's the total capital need?**
₹30.4 Cr to EBITDA breakeven in the base case, ₹42.9 Cr in the aggressive
case. The ₹210 Cr full funding plan funds national distribution, which is a
growth choice, not a survival requirement.

**91. What does the seed money do?**
Two-city launch, three SKUs, D2C and quick commerce, first production runs,
and the team hires — specifically FMCG distribution.

**92. What's the dilution path?**
Founders 90% → 72% post-seed → 56.5% post-A → 47.4% post-B. ESOP refreshed
2% at each institutional round.

**93. When is Series A?**
End of Year 2 at ~₹15 Cr revenue run-rate, ₹250 Cr post-money.

**94. What are the milestones between seed and Series A?**
Modern trade entry, full range rollout, ₹15 Cr revenue run-rate, and M3
retention sustained above 35% at scale.

## I. Exit (95–100)

**95. What's the exit path?**
Strategic acquisition. Coca-Cola India, PepsiCo India, ITC, Dabur, Wipro
Consumer, or a global strategic entering India.

**96. What multiple?**
3.9× revenue — the multiple PepsiCo actually paid for Poppi per its Form
10-Q, not a comparable-company average.

**97. What are the modelled outcomes?**
Y7 exit: ₹1,209 Cr conservative, ₹2,964 Cr base, ₹5,655 Cr aggressive.

**98. What does seed return?**
15.9× conservative, 39.0× base, 74.5× aggressive. Probability-weighted with
a 55% failure rate: **13.6× MOIC, 45.1% IRR**.

**99. Why would a strategic buy rather than build?**
Speed and brand. Building a credible Indian-flavour functional portfolio
takes 3–5 years and carries execution risk. Buying the category leader
removes both. It is exactly the logic PepsiCo applied to Poppi.

**100. Is an IPO realistic?**
Not at these numbers. ₹760 Cr of Year 7 revenue is below the scale at which
an Indian consumer IPO makes sense. Trade sale is the base case; IPO is a
Year 10+ conversation contingent on reaching ₹2,000 Cr+.
