**₹37.14 of every can is tax.** Before the brand, the distributor or the
retailer is paid anything. In modern trade, FROLIC banks ₹61.57 of a ₹130
shelf price — **47%**.

This single fact reorders the strategy:

- **D2C is worth 36% more per can than modern trade.** Not a "channel to
  graduate out of" — the most profitable channel, permanently. The
  subscription programme exists because of this table.
- **Quick commerce is the scale compromise.** 25% commission hurts, but it
  reaches the target consumer at a cost-to-serve below modern trade.
- **Retail is a brand-building expense that happens to break even.** Its
  value is visibility and credibility, not contribution.

Larger formats carry slightly better margin — fibre and liquid scale with
volume, but the can end, label and conversion are near-fixed.

**Recommendation: launch 250 ml only.** The margin gain from 330 ml (1.1
points) does not justify a second SKU's tooling, inventory and shelf
complexity for a pre-revenue brand. 250 ml also matches the price point
consumers accept for a single-serve premium beverage and matches the funded
incumbent's format, which makes shelf comparison straightforward. Add 330 ml
in Year 3 for HoReCa and multipack.

## Minimum viable selling price

The MRP at which a can clears COGS plus a 50% gross margin, after all
downstream leakage. Solved analytically — every deduction except per-can
logistics is proportional to price.

| Format | Planned MRP | Net required | **Minimum viable MRP** | Headroom |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 250 ml | ₹130 | ₹68.00 | **₹115.89** | 10.9% |
| 300 ml | ₹145 | ₹75.00 | **₹127.23** | 12.3% |
| 330 ml | ₹155 | ₹79.60 | **₹134.69** | 13.4% |

> **Only 11% of headroom.** FROLIC cannot run deep discounts, cannot fight a
> price war, and cannot sustain "buy one get one" mechanics. A 20% off
> promotion on a 250 ml can puts it below the viability floor.
>
> This is a strategic constraint, and it is also a moat: the same arithmetic
> applies to every entrant. Nobody can buy this market with price. The
> category will be won on taste, brand and distribution — which is the
> competition FROLIC would choose.

## The 35-point tax lever

Carbonated sweetened beverages: **40% GST.**
Non-carbonated fruit/juice-based beverages and packaged water: **5% GST.**

A still FROLIC — same fibre, same Indian flavours, no carbonation — would
face 5% GST. At the same ₹130 MRP the ex-tax value rises from ₹92.86 to
₹123.81: **+₹30.95 per can, straight to the value chain.**

That is larger than the entire Year-5 COGS reduction programme, achievable
immediately, and requires no scale.

**This is not a recommendation to abandon soda.** Carbonation is the category
and the product premise. But a still line — a functional Indian cooler, in the
aam panna and kokum territory FROLIC already owns — is:

- a materially higher-margin second act
- a hedge if carbonated tax policy tightens further
- a way to reach consumers who reject fizz
- a channel into HoReCa and institutional where carbonation is a drawback

It belongs on the Series A roadmap, and the fact that this pack identifies it
is itself a signal about how the business is being modelled.
