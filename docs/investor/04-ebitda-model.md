## What drives the turn to profit

Three things move together, and all three are required:

1. **Gross margin 55.7% → 66.7%.** COGS falls ₹34 → ₹23 per can as volume
   moves from co-packer tolling to committed annual can procurement. This is
   the most reliable of the three — it is a procurement outcome, not a
   consumer one.
2. **Marketing 60% → 18% of net revenue.** This is the *least* reliable. It
   assumes brand equity and repeat carry demand that paid media carried in
   Year 1. If marketing has to stay at 30% in Year 5, EBITDA drops to
   ~₹30 Cr and margin halves to ~11%.
3. **Opex leverage.** ₹68 Cr of opex on ₹267 Cr of revenue is 25% — lean but
   achievable for a brand that outsources manufacturing.

| Y5 | ₹73.18 Cr | 66.7% | **+₹4.27 Cr** | +5.8% |

This case assumes management **responds** to underperformance — slower
hiring, deferred line investment, tighter G&A. Opex peaks at ₹27 Cr rather
than ₹68 Cr. A conservative case that keeps base-case spending is a strawman,
not a downside.

The honest read: the conservative case is a **survivable business, not a
venture outcome.** ₹73 Cr of revenue and ₹4 Cr of EBITDA at Year 5 is a real
company that would struggle to raise a Series B at a step-up. Seed investors
still return 15.9× because they entered at ₹50 Cr; Series B investors return
1.13×. That asymmetry is the whole risk profile of this deal.
