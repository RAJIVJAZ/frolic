# 10 · Siting & Raw Material Sourcing

Written after the founder's base was corrected from Pune to **Prayagraj, Uttar
Pradesh**, and in response to the stated plan: *build around Prayagraj for now,
then relocate to wherever raw material is cheapest.*

The analysis does not support the second half of that plan as stated. It may
support a smaller version of it.

---

## 1. The finding that changes the question

**Fruit and botanical inputs are roughly 13% of COGS.** The aluminium can is
35% — nearly three times more.

| Component | ₹ / can (Y1) | Share of COGS |
|---|---|---|
| **Can body** | ₹12.00 | **35.3%** |
| Flavour base, acidulants, salt, sweetener | ₹8.00 | 23.5% |
| Conversion / co-packer tolling | ₹5.50 | 16.2% |
| Prebiotic fibre | ₹4.00 | 11.8% |
| Secondary packaging | ₹2.50 | 7.4% |
| Can end | ₹1.50 | 4.4% |
| Water, CO₂ | ₹0.50 | 1.5% |

Of the ₹8.00 flavour base, actual fruit and botanical content is perhaps ₹4.40
— the rest is acidulants, salt and sweetener, none of which vary by geography.

**So a 30% reduction in fruit cost saves ₹1.32 per can — 3.9% of COGS.**

That is not nothing. But it is a fraction of what the same effort spent on can
procurement would return, and it can be entirely erased by freight if the plant
ends up further from the market. Relocating a business to optimise 13% of COGS
is the wrong shape of decision unless the other 87% comes along for the ride.

> **Restate the objective.** Not "cheapest raw material" but **lowest total
> landed cost per can delivered to the consumer** — fruit, plus can, plus
> conversion, plus freight to market, plus the cost of not being near a capable
> co-packer.

## 2. Where FROLIC's inputs actually grow

| SKU | Key agricultural input | Where it comes from | From Prayagraj |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Guava Chili** | Pink guava | **Prayagraj itself** — Allahabad Surkha is GI-tagged, ~1,000 ha across Kaushambi and Kaurihar-II | **On the doorstep** |
| **Orange Masala** | Amla | **Pratapgarh, adjacent district** — ~40% of India's entire aonla crop | **~60 km** |
| **Jamun Pop** | Jamun | Central and northern India, including UP | **Local to regional** |
| **Aam Panna Spark** | Raw green mango | UP is a major mango state (Malihabad belt, near Lucknow) | **~200 km** |
| **Nimbu Masala**, **Himalayan Lemon** | Lime / hill lemon | Widely grown; hill lemon from Himachal and Uttarakhand | Moderate |
| **Ginger Lime Lift** | Fresh ginger | Kerala, North-East, some UP | Long haul |
| **Jeera Fizz** | Cumin | Gujarat and Rajasthan (~80%+ of national crop) | Long haul, but a dry spice — cheap to ship, long shelf life |
| **Kala Khatta Rush** | Tamarind, black grape | Widely grown | Moderate |
| **Kokum Burst** | Kokum | **Konkan coast only** — Ratnagiri, Sindhudurg, Goa, coastal Karnataka | **~1,400 km. Structural.** |

### What this says

**Prayagraj is not a compromise location. For three SKUs it is close to
optimal.** The founder is sitting inside India's GI-tagged guava belt, 60 km
from the district that grows 40% of the national amla crop, in good jamun
country. That is a sourcing position most beverage founders would have to
relocate *to*.

**Kokum is the one genuine outlier**, and no plant location fixes it: it grows
on one coastline and nowhere else. Kokum Burst will always carry a freight
penalty from any northern base. That is an argument about *that SKU*, not about
the company's address — and it is a reason to keep Kokum out of the launch set,
which it already is not in.

## 3. What a relocation should actually be tested against

If the relocation question is revisited later, these are the variables that
matter, in order of impact:

| Factor | Why it outranks fruit cost |
|---|---|
| **Co-packer availability** | Sleek-can carbonated capability is scarce and clustered. Conversion is ₹5.50/can and falls to ₹2.90 with committed volume — a bigger prize than fruit, and entirely dependent on partner access |
| **Freight to market** | The premium consumer is in metros. Every extra 500 km to Delhi NCR or Mumbai is added cost on every can, forever |
| **Can supply** | 35% of COGS. Can makers ship nationally, so this is a volume negotiation, not a geography one — which is exactly why moving for fruit is the wrong lever |
| **State incentives** | UP's food processing policy offers capital subsidy on plant and machinery. Worth quantifying before assuming another state is cheaper |
| **Cold storage & seasonality** | Jamun has a ~six-week window; guava and mango are seasonal. Proximity to the orchard only helps if there is storage to hold the crop |
| **Talent** | A food technologist and an FMCG sales lead are easier to hire in an established food-processing corridor |
| **Fruit price** | Real, but 13% of COGS — and partly capturable by contracting rather than relocating |

---

## 4. Recommendation

**Phase 1 — stay in Prayagraj.** Not as a compromise, but because the sourcing
position for guava, amla and jamun is genuinely good and the launch range can
be built around inputs that are local or regional.

**Do not relocate to chase fruit cost.** The saving available is ~4% of COGS
and is smaller than the freight and co-packer penalties a poorly-chosen
location would introduce.

**Do revisit siting at the point of owning capacity** — a Series B decision,
not a pre-seed one. At that point the question is where to put a line, and the
inputs to that decision are co-packer displacement, market freight, state
incentives and can logistics, with fruit cost as a secondary term.

**Secure the local advantage instead.** Forward contracts with guava growers in
the Kaushambi belt and amla suppliers in Pratapgarh are cheap to establish now
and are a genuine barrier to a fast follower — a Mumbai-based competitor cannot
easily replicate them.

## 5. Consequences for the rest of the plan

| Document | Change |
|---|---|
| Go-to-market phasing | Phase 1 was Mumbai + Bengaluru. From Prayagraj the natural first markets are **Delhi NCR (~600 km)** and **Lucknow (~200 km)** — Lucknow as a low-cost proving ground, Delhi NCR as the first real premium market |
| Beachhead sizing | **Unchanged.** The premium consumer is still in the five metros; manufacturing location does not move the customers |
| Freight assumptions | The model's per-can logistics figures were built assuming metro-proximate manufacturing. **They need revisiting once a co-packer location is known** — flagged rather than silently adjusted, because a new number without a real quotation would be false precision |
| Registered office | Prayagraj, Uttar Pradesh |
